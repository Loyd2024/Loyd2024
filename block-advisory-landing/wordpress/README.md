# block.ke: development property page and payment calculators

`block-development-page.php` is a single WPCode PHP snippet that improves block.ke property pages in four ways. Everything it shows is read from the listing, so a development keeps working like any other block.ke property: same URL, site header and menu, search and maps, agent, similar listings and SEO. To update the page, edit the property as usual.

## 1. Payment calculators on every for-sale property page

| Listing | Calculator |
|---|---|
| **Off-plan**: Property Status *Off-Plan / Ongoing* and Completion not *Ready now* | **Payment plan calculator**: deposit, then monthly or quarterly instalments up to completion. It replaces the theme's mortgage calculator in the same place on the page. |
| **Complete**: Status *Complete*, or Completion *Ready now* | The theme's mortgage calculator, unchanged. |

The instalment calculator fills itself in from the listing:

- **Price:** the listing price. Visitors can change it.
- **Deposit and reservation fee:** read from the description's *Payment Plan* section, for example "Deposit: 20% of the purchase price" and "Reservation fee: KES 200,000". If the listing states no deposit, the calculator uses 20% and says so.
- **Months:** from today until the completion date. The date comes from `bke_completion_stated`, a "Completion" or "Occupation" line in the description, or the Completion year. With no date, visitors choose a period of 6 to 60 months.

Its button opens the agent contact form with "Please send me the official payment plan" already typed in.

**Mortgage defaults need updating.** The theme's mortgage calculator currently assumes 4.125% interest over 30 years, which are US figures. Update them in *WpResidence Options → Property Page → Mortgage calculator*, for example to 13% over 20 years.

## 2. Amenities and nearby places on every property page

These two changes apply to the theme's own (classic) property pages.

**Features & Amenities** opens with up to six amenity tiles, as on the development layout: navy tiles with a brass icon, the amenity's name and a one-line description. Everything else follows as one checklist, titled "The everyday essentials" when it holds only security, parking, lifts, water and power, otherwise "Also included". This replaces the theme's list of feature groups, where most groups held one to three items under their own heading. The items are the listing's *Features & Amenities* ticks, as before, so there is nothing new to fill in.

**Address** gains a *What's nearby* panel. It lists the nearest two business districts, malls, schools or universities, hospitals, parks and airports, with straight-line distances from the listing's map pin. The places are a curated list of 36 Nairobi landmarks in `blockke_dev_landmarks()`, and more can be added there, for example Gateway Mall for Syokimau listings. A group appears only when one of its places is close enough to matter: 6 km for malls and parks, 8 km for schools and hospitals (5 km for universities), and further for business districts and airports. There is no panel for listings outside Nairobi, without a map pin, or with the pin left on the theme's default map centre. To show your own list instead, fill in **Places nearby** in the listing's *Development page* box, one per line as `Place | travel time`.

Both are moved into place by a small script at the end of the page. Without JavaScript, or if the theme's markup changes, the theme's own content stays as it was.

## 3. Development layout (switched on per listing)

A landing-page layout for new developments, drawn between the site's normal header and footer.

| Section | Comes from |
|---|---|
| Hero: name, area, status, *From* price, completion, photo, two-field enquiry form | Title, Area, Property Status, lowest unit price, completion date, featured image. A ready single home reads *Price*, unless its price label ("before" label) is *From*. |
| Key facts strip | The description's *at a Glance* or *Project Snapshot* table or list, plus unit sizes. A single home (a unit page or a one-home listing) also shows its bedrooms, bathrooms, built area and plot (in m², like the sizes). |
| Overview and *Read the full description* | The description's opening paragraphs and its *Why …* list. A short title-like first line ("3 Bedroom Villa at Tilisi Views, Limuru") is left out, since the page heading already says it. The full description stays on the page, collapsed; on phones its wide tables swipe sideways, with a shadow on the side that has more. |
| Photos | The listing gallery, with each photo's Media Library caption when it has one |
| Residences table, with filters | The description's Unit / Size / Price table (a price column may also be headed *From* or *Starting*), linked to the unit listings. A word after the price, such as *cash* or *instalment*, shows under it. With no table, the unit listings (Multi Units) are used. |
| Amenities | Up to six feature tiles for the stand-out amenities (pool, sky lounge, gym, cinema and so on), each with an icon and a one-line description, then a checklist of everything else. The items come from bold-titled groups in the description's *Amenities* section, otherwise the *Features & Amenities* ticks. |
| Payment plan | Off-plan: the *Payment Plan* steps (also when *Payment Plan* is an H3 under *Pricing*) plus the instalment calculator. A plan with a fixed share per instalment ("10% every 3 months") is modelled that way, with the balance on completion. With no plan stated and completion three months away or less, a short note and the *Get the official payment plan* button replace the estimate. Complete: a mortgage calculator. When the listing offers an instalment plan the text says so, and *Payment plan text* replaces it. *Calculator price* starts the calculator from another figure, such as the mortgage price when the cash price shown is lower; the field then reads *Mortgage price*. |
| Location | Map pin from the listing coordinates, plus places and travel times from the *Location* section. When one *Amenities & Location* paragraph feeds both sections, the travel sentences go to Location and the rest to Amenities, each in whole sentences. An intro sentence that only repeats a place row ("30 km from Nairobi CBD" over a *Nairobi CBD · 30 km* row) is left out. With none listed, the nearest business district, mall, school, hospital, park and airport, with straight-line distances. |
| FAQ | The description's *FAQ* / *Frequently Asked Questions* section, also as an H3 inside another section. Rank Math and Yoast FAQ blocks are read too. |
| Similar developments | Other developments for sale in the same area |
| Enquiry form and advisor | The listing's agent |

**Look and feel.** The page uses block.ke's warm off-white with sand-coloured sections. Navy is used for the key facts strip, the hero enquiry card, Amenities and *Why Block*, with brass accents. Headings are set in Instrument Serif, a Google Font, and a few words in each are picked out in brass italics, for example "What comes *with the keys.*". To accent part of the overview headline override, wrap those words in asterisks: `About *the building*`. Setting `display_font` to `''` returns all headings to the site font.

**Amenity tiles.** Tiles are chosen by kind, best first, and appear in full rows of 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6. The kinds are: beach, pool, sky lounge or sky garden, cinema, spa, gym, nature, kids' play, café, golf, gardens, yoga, shops, co-working, sports courts, BBQ, residents' lounge and jogging track. A tile keeps the listing's own name when it is short. A longer item becomes the tile's description under a short title, for example "Sky garden" over "Terrace sky garden with panoramic city views". Security, parking, lifts, power and water go in the checklist, titled "The everyday essentials" when that is all it holds.

**Amenity photos.** Give a listing's photos a caption in the Media Library (*Media → the photo → Caption*), for example "Indoor heated pool", "Sky Lounge" or "Private cinema". Photos whose caption names an amenity then appear as a captioned photo strip at the top of Amenities, before the tiles. On phones it swipes; on desktop it shows one full row of 2, 3 or 4 photos (6 or 8 in two rows). Tapping a photo opens the gallery on it. Other captions, such as "Lantana Road frontage", show on the gallery photos and in the photo viewer. With fewer than two amenity captions, the section looks as before. The caption is WordPress's own field, so the theme may also show it wherever it displays captions.

**Turn it on for one listing.** Edit the property, find the **Development page** box, and set **Layout: On**. The box also has optional overrides: hero line, key facts, deposit, reservation fee, payment plan text, calculator price, places, photo labels, brochure PDF, video and similar listings.

**Photo labels (v1.3.2).** Off-plan pages label the main photo "Project render" and the gallery "Project renders.". For show-house or site photos, set **Main photo label** (e.g. *Show apartment*) and **Gallery note** (e.g. *Photos of the show apartment.*) in the box, or the custom fields `bke_dev_hero_label` and `bke_dev_gallery_note`. "none" hides either.

**Preview before switching on.** While logged in, add `?bke_layout=1` to any property URL, or use the admin-bar menu *Development layout → Preview*. Use `?bke_layout=0` to see the classic page.

**Turn it on for every development at once.** Set the option `blockke_dev_page['auto'] = true`. Every listing with units (`property_has_subunits = 1`) then uses the layout unless its box says *Off*.

## 4. Ad landing pages (for Google Ads and other campaigns)

Every for-sale listing has a landing page for paid campaigns, with nothing to set up: its URL plus `?lp=1`, for example `https://block.ke/property/santorini-residences-westlands-1-4-bed-apartments-lofts/?lp=1`. For a tidier address, create a page under the private page *Landing pages* (slug `lp`) and add a custom field `bke_lp_listing` holding the listing's ID. Santorini's is ready as a draft: `https://block.ke/lp/santorini-residences/` (page 42708, listing 40401); publish it once the v1.3 snippet is on. On these pages, tick Rank Math's *No Index* so they stay out of the sitemap, and set the listing's main photo as the featured image so WhatsApp and Facebook link previews show it. The page's own text appears only if the snippet is switched off, so a line linking to the listing is a good fallback.

It shows the development layout's content, built for visitors arriving from an ad:

- **A slim header** with the logo, phone, WhatsApp and *Get the price list*, instead of the site menu. The footer holds only the address, contact details and privacy policy.
- **No ways off the page but contact.** Breadcrumbs, the share button, *Similar developments*, the unit "Details" links and links inside the description and FAQ are left out.
- **Phone first.** A tall photo carries the status, area, development name, its homes and street (for example "1 – 3 Bed & Lofts · Lantana Road") and the price, completion and deposit. The two-field form follows straight after, with its "Sent straight to your WhatsApp" line, and the whole form, button included, fits the first screen of an iPhone with Safari's or Chrome's bars showing (390×664) and of most Android phones. The advisor's photo and name sit under the form, and the WhatsApp and price list bar appears once the form scrolls away.
- **Hidden from search** (`noindex, follow`), so it never competes with the listing in Google results.
- **Tracking as usual.** The page runs WordPress's head and footer hooks, so Google Ads conversions (form, WhatsApp and call clicks), GA4, Meta Pixel, Zoho PageSense and SalesIQ load as on every page. Leads carry the ad's gclid and UTM tags, and the lead email shows the landing page address.

Rentals never become landing pages. Set `landing_pages` to `false` to switch them off.

**Who it suits best.** Both layouts now show the description's "Who … suits best" list, its note on who it suits less well, and the first three sentences of its "… for Investors" section with a button to ask for rental and resale comparables.

## 5. Area pages (v1.5, switched on per area)

A Neighbourhood page (`/property_area/lavington/`) on the same layout, plus a page for each kind of listing in it (`/property_area/lavington/villas-for-sale/`). One rule runs through both: **a grid only ever holds one kind of listing.** Homes, commercial property and land are never mixed, and nor are sale and rent.

**The area page** shows one section for each kind that has listings: homes for sale, homes to rent, commercial for sale, commercial to rent, land for sale, land to lease. Each shows up to 6 cards (`area_cards`) and "See all", with chips for the types inside it (Apartments 27 · Studios 3). The hero names what is there ("Homes for sale from KES 4.2M, homes to rent from KES 120K a month and commercial property for sale from KES 13M.") with chips that jump to each section. Then come nearby areas with the same kind of listing (config `neighbours`), the Neighbourhood's own description from **Properties → Neighbourhoods** under *About Lavington* (first paragraph, then "Read more"; on the area page only, so the 20 segment pages don't repeat it), *Why Block*, and the enquiry form. The area page keeps its own Rank Math title and description.

**Segment pages**, 20 of them, each holding one kind and Buy or Rent:
- homes: `homes-`, `apartments-`, `villas-`, `houses-` and `studios-for-sale` / `-for-rent`;
- commercial: `commercial-`, `offices-`, `warehouses-` and `shops-for-sale` / `-for-rent`;
- land: `land-for-sale`, `land-for-rent` ("Land for lease").

A segment page lists every match, 24 at a time with "Show more" (`area_page_size`), with bedroom chips for homes and a sort (recommended, price either way, newest). Under the list, "Also in Lavington" links to the other kinds. When a segment is empty the page says so and offers "Ask an advisor to find one".

**What goes in a grid.** Sold and let listings are left out. A development is one card (its main listing, else its cheapest matching home; two listings of the same Project share it), priced *From* its cheapest matching home, with its bedroom range and how many homes. A listing for several homes reads its bedrooms from the title ("Studio, 1-Bed & 2-Bed" → *Studio–2 bed*) and shows no single size. Kind comes from the category: land categories are land; commercial, offices, warehouses and shops are commercial unless the listing is also a home with bedrooms (a flat above shops stays a home); villas and houses by their category; studios by the Studio category. Buy or Rent follows the similar-homes rules: a listing tagged both and priced by the month is a rental, and a home whose price contradicts its tag is left out. A per-sq-ft price is shown as such, sorts last, and is left out of the "from … to …" range (a commercial segment that mixes the two shows no range).

**Search engines.** A segment page has its own title ("Villas for Sale in Lavington, Nairobi | Block Real Estate", dropping ", Nairobi" past 60 characters), description ("Homes for sale in Westlands, Nairobi: 28 listings from KES 4.2M to KES 23.5M. …"), canonical and Open Graph tags, set through Rank Math's filters (and printed directly when Rank Math is off). Rank Math's rel next/prev is switched off on these pages. Canonicals: a type page that holds exactly what its parent holds (all homes for sale in Kilimani are apartments) points at the parent, and a kind that is all an area has and fits on the area page points at the area page, so the two never compete. A page is `noindex, follow` when the page its canonical names has fewer than 3 listings (`area_index_min`), or fewer than 5 for type pages such as apartments or offices (`area_type_index_min`); the area page itself is always indexable. Unknown segment names are a 404; a segment URL for an area that is not switched on goes to the area page.

**On phones** the hero form and key facts are left out so the listings come straight after the heading (on a segment page the first card starts on the first screen), each section ends with its own "See all" link, and the bottom bar ("Ask an advisor", WhatsApp) appears once past the hero. Commercial and land pages say "property" or "land" rather than "homes", link "Search" to the site search instead of the homes search sheet, and their forms ask no bedrooms.

**Enquiries** go through the same endpoint with no listing, the area and the segment. The email reads "[Block] Area enquiry: Jane - Villas for sale in Lavington", with what they are looking for (Buy or Rent, type, bedrooms, budget). Zoho gets the same text in the description; its budget field is left empty, as its picklist uses the price-check popup's bands. The enquiries inbox shows them with a one-line summary ("Buy · Villa · 4 bed · KES 60M and above").

**Turning it on.** Off by default. Logged-in editors can preview any area with `?bke_area=1` (and `?bke_area=0` for the classic page), also from the admin bar's "Area page" menu. Then set `area_pages` in the option to a list of area slugs, e.g. `array( 'lavington', 'westlands' )`, or `true` for all. The segment URLs need WordPress's rewrite rules refreshed; the snippet does it by itself whenever its rule is missing from the stored rules (first run, or after the rules were rebuilt while the snippet was off). After switching the snippet off for good, save **Settings → Permalinks** once so the segment URLs stop resolving. Each area's data is cached (`bkd_area_{term id}`) and rebuilt whenever a listing changes, as similar homes are.

**Tracking.** `area_filter`, `area_sort`, `area_more`, `area_card_click` (with position and section), plus the usual enquiry events with `area_page` set to the segment.

## 6. New developments, search results and one card family (v1.6, every part switched off)

One card for a development everywhere a list of them appears (the new developments page, area and segment pages, search results), one set of filters with the same words and the same address parameters, and a calmer way from a card to the development and back. Each part has its own switch and an editor preview, so it can be checked on block.ke before anyone else sees it.

**The card.** A 3:2 photo, the name in the display serif, a place line ("Brookside, Westlands · Studio to 3 bed") and a data line ("From KES 6.18M · Completion Apr 2028"). No badges, ribbons, icons or "Images on request" tiles. A development without a cover photo is never drawn as a placeholder: it is listed by name under the grid instead, and a photo that fails to load leaves the plain photo box. Prices are rounded up to three figures (KES 6.06M, KES 25.3M, KES 180K a month), and read "From" whenever the card covers more than one price or more than one size ("1–4 bed · From KES 6M"). Bedrooms come from the homes listed, else from the title, so "2, 3, 4 & 5 Bed" reads "2–5 bed". A development is one card: a main listing and its units, else listings sharing a Project, else one listing; two groups for the same building (same Project, or the same name in the same main area) are merged. The card opens the development's listing for sale (on the new layout when it has one); in a rent section it opens one of its rentals.

**New developments page** (`dev_index`, preview `?bke_index=1`; page `dev_index_page`, default 30752 at `/new-developments/`). Every development selling off-plan, by completion year. The standfirst counts those in Nairobi and nearby (the cities in `index_metro`) and says how many are elsewhere in Kenya ("200 developments selling off-plan in Nairobi and nearby, and 4 elsewhere in Kenya, from KES 1.87M"). Then a filter bar (area, bedrooms, price, completion, sort), a lead development and two picks (`index_picks`, else chosen from those with the most photos and a large cover, rotating weekly; shown only on the unfiltered, recommended view, and hidden as soon as a filter or sort is set), groups for this year, the next two and "{year} and later", each showing 8 and then "Show N more completing in 2027", then "Completion date to be confirmed" for dates that have passed or were never given. Then *Ready to move into* (the first 8), the one navy band with the shortlist form and WhatsApp, and an A–Z of every development by neighbourhood, busiest first, so each one is a plain link in the page; an area with one development, and developments without an area, are listed together under "Other areas" at the end, each name with its area. "Ready now" in the completion filter shows the whole Ready set as one grid, narrowed by the other filters like the years. A sort or "Ready now" flattens the page into one grid, 18 cards at a time; with a price filter set, cards show their price range ("KES 6.5M–12.5M") instead of "From". The server draws every view from the address (filters, sort, `n`, open years), so a reload, Back or a visit without JavaScript shows the same cards the script would; the script then filters in place and keeps the address up to date, leaving campaign tags (`gclid`, `utm_*`) in it. On phones and tablets "Filters" opens a full-screen dialog above the theme header (Tab stays inside it; Escape, Close or widening the screen closes it). A development reaches the groups when it is homes for sale, off-plan, has a cover, at least 3 photos (`index_min_photos`), a price and an area; anything else is in the A–Z only, and one with neither an area nor a status is on no list until tagged (the admin bar lists these as "Not shown"). An editor can add one sentence to a development (post meta `bke_index_line`, up to 90 characters). The page's title, description, canonical, share image (the lead's cover) and JSON-LD (ItemList and BreadcrumbList, no prices) are its own; with Rank Math, its breadcrumb replaces Rank Math's, the page is a CollectionPage and the default Article node is dropped. Any filter, sort or paging in the address is `noindex, follow` with the canonical on the plain page, and the "Show more" links are `nofollow`. Shortlist enquiries arrive as "Form: Shortlist request (new developments page)", with the visitor's filters ("Browsing: 2 bed developments in Westlands") and budget in the filter's words. While the page is on, snippet 41011's list of completion pages leaves it out (through the `blockke_completion` option filter; 41011 itself is not edited).

**Area and segment pages on the new card** (`card_v16`, preview `?bke_cards=1`). The v1.5 pages keep their routes and titles and change look. Counts are of homes (a development listed without its homes counts as one; commercial property and land count listings), and each appears once. The area page opens with photos of the leading developments of its first kind (three, one large and two stacked; one when it has only a few), and that section's cards start after them, so no photo shows twice. Then one section per kind, its homes under the heading ("275 homes, from KES 4.2M"), type links ("All · Apartments 234 · Villas 2 · Studios 32") and up to 6 cards in whole rows of three (on phones one card, then the next three as rows, or all of them when the page holds every one); "See all homes for sale in Westlands" only where some are left out at that width, and developments without a photo by name under the cards once the rest are shown. A short form follows the first section ("Price lists for Westlands"), then nearby areas as a list with their homes, the area's description and the navy band. A segment page counts its homes in the standfirst ("275 homes, from KES 4.2M to KES 222M."), has the same type links and a bar like the new developments page's: bedrooms (homes), price (sale or rent bands), completion (homes for sale) and sort, held in view as the list scrolls, with "Filters" opening a sheet on phones and tablets, "Clear filters", and a count only while a filter is set ("12 matches"). With a price filter cards show their price range; when nothing matches the page says what was asked ("No 4+ bed homes for sale in Westlands under KES 8M completing in 2027.") and offers the filters to drop, most results first; after "Show more" the keyboard focus is on the first new card. Developments without a photo are listed under "Also listed (4)". There is no bar when there is nothing to choose. The bar works without JavaScript too: the server applies the filters and sort from the address, so a shared or reloaded link is right before any script runs, and the script then keeps the address up to date. Filtered or sorted views are `noindex, follow` with the canonical on the plain segment page. A kind that is all an area has points its canonical at the area page only when every development of it is there (in the photos, the cards or the names under them), and nearby areas link to each page at its canonical. Editors can see the new cards on block.ke before visitors do with `?bke_cards=1` (and `?bke_cards=0` for the v1.5 ones) on any area page that is on (add `?bke_area=1` for one that is not), also from the admin bar's "Area page" menu; links between area pages keep the preview. Switching `card_v16` off brings back the v1.5 pages unchanged.

**Search results** (`search_layout`) are described in section 7, and the way from a card to the development and back in section 8.

**Development pages while `card_v16` is on.** "See all homes for sale in Westlands" under similar homes leads to that segment page (at its canonical) instead of the search results, and the page's search links are `nofollow`, as the area pages' are. With `card_v16` off they are as in v1.5.

**Turning it on**, in this order, purging Cloudflare for the pages concerned after each: `card_v16`, then `view_transitions` and `prefetch`, then `dev_index`, then `search_layout`, then `search_redirect`. The rows behind every list are read once into the transient `bkn_rows` (developments in `bkn_devs`, the index in `bkn_index`) and rebuilt when a listing changes, as similar homes are (including its layout switch, `bke_index_line` and photos, even when written without a post save), or when the settings they read change (`enabled`, `auto`, `auto_units`, `neighbours`, `studio_cat`). A rebuild that finds no listings at all never replaces a good copy, and works without snippet 41011's Completion taxonomy.

**Tracking.** `index_filter`, `index_sort`, `index_more`, `index_no_results` (once per search; from the phone sheet only when it is applied), `area_filter`, `area_sort`, `area_more`, `area_card_click`, search's own filter and card events, `context_beds`, and `back_to_results` with where the visitor came from.

## 7. Search results (v1.6, switched off)

`/advanced-search/` (page 9; config `search_page`) on the new layout, built from the cached listing rows rather than a new database search, so snippet 41011's completion filter is never involved. Nothing changes until `search_layout` is on; editors can preview any search with `?bke_search=1` (and `?bke_search=0` for the theme's page), also from the admin bar's "Search results" menu.

**Which searches it shows.** It reads the theme's own fields (`property_action_category[]`, `property_category[]`, `completion`), the aliases in `search_params` (area: `location`, `property_area`, `advanced_area`, `adv_location`; bedrooms: `componentsbeds`, `beds`, `bedrooms`; price: `price_low`, `price_min`, `price_max`), and its own clean ones: `for` (sale, rent), `type` (homes, apartments, villas, houses, studios, commercial, offices, warehouses, shops, land, or a segment slug such as `apartments-for-sale`), `area` (a slug, a comma list, or `other`), `beds` (studio, 1 to 4, 4 meaning four and more), `price` (`8-12` in KES millions, rent bands in thousands), `completion` (41011's slugs), `sort` and `n`. Areas are found by slug, then by name, so "Westlands" from the search sheet works. Empty fields, "all", "any", the price slider's ends (0 and 500,000,000) and the keys in `search_ignore` (campaign tags, `submit`) are ignored. **Anything else, such as a keyword, an MLS number, a saved search, an area or category the site doesn't have, or a malformed value, hands the page back to the theme exactly as today.** The admin bar says which key did it ("This search stays classic: it uses "keyword""), so a theme field we don't know yet can be added to `search_ignore` or `search_params`. A PHP error or empty listing rows also fall back to the theme.

**The page.** Breadcrumb, an H1 written from the search in the area pages' words ("2 bed apartments for sale in Westlands", "Homes for rent", "Land for lease", "Property for sale and rent in Westlands"), and the count once beneath it: "108 homes in 90 developments" when some cards hold several homes ("12 listings in 10 projects" for land), just "5 homes" when each card is one home, with a "Map" link to `/search/` carrying the search in its own parameters (`act`, `q`, `type`, `bd`, `pmin`, `pmax`) while that page is published (config `search_map_page`, default 39161). Type tabs as underlined text ("All homes · Apartments · Villas · Houses · Studios", plus Commercial and Land when the search finds them). Then one section per kind and Buy or Rent, homes first, then commercial, then land, sale before rent, each headed "Homes for sale", "Commercial for rent", "Land for lease" and so on (the heading is kept for screen readers only when there is one section). Never mixed.

**Cards** use the v1.6 card family. A development is one card, linking to its main listing, with the homes in it that match: "2 homes match: 2 and 3 bed" (just "2 homes match" under a search for one size), up to three rows of size and price that link to each home, and "See all 4 in the price list" when there are more. A listing for several sizes ("Studio, 1-Bed & 2-Bed Apartments") stands for its development and is never listed as one home, and the card's bedrooms include the sizes in such titles. A development's only listing in a section (a single home, or one flat to rent in a building selling off-plan) is set like every other card, so a row never mixes two kinds: its building's name, then "Riverside · 3 bed · 160 m²", then "KES 12.5M · Completion 2027", and it opens that home. With one area searched, place lines leave the area out, as on the area pages. With a price filter the card shows the range of the homes that match ("KES 8M–22.5M"). A listing with no photo at all is a line under "Also listed (3)" at the foot of its section, never a placeholder tile. "New on Block" goes on at most 1 card in 5. 18 cards per section (`index_page_size`), then "Show 18 more": a link to `?n=36` that, with JavaScript, adds the next cards in place and keeps `n` in the address, so a reload or Back shows the same list.

**Matching.** Buy or Rent and kind follow the area pages' rules. Bedrooms follow `beds_mode` (4 means four and more). A price band takes homes priced inside it; a development's main listing matches when its from–to range overlaps the band; per-sq-ft prices are left out while a price is set. Completion uses the listing's Completion term, else its development's stated date; Ready now takes completed buildings. Recommended order is the theme's: featured, then newest. Also "Price, lowest first", "Price, highest first" and "Newest on Block".

## 8. From a card to a development and back (v1.6, switched off)

What happens when a visitor opens a development from a v1.6 card (the new developments index, search results, and area and segment pages once they use the new cards) and comes back.

**The development remembers the list.** A plain click on a card (not a new tab) keeps a note for that browser tab: which card, the list's address and name, and the bedrooms the visitor was looking at. Card links stay clean permalinks. For the next 30 minutes, on that development:
- the residences filter starts on the visitor's bedrooms, with "Showing 2 bed homes only. Show all" above the table ("Showing studios only", and "… from your search." when the card was on search results), only when the table has that filter. As on the lists, 4 means four and more, so a table that also has 5 bed homes stays on All without a note;
- the link above the name reads "Back to new developments" (or "Back to Westlands", "Back to homes for sale in Westlands", "Back to your search"), and still steps back in the browser's history, so the list comes back exactly as it was. From 700px it heads the breadcrumb on a line of its own; it is 44px to tap. Without a note the existing "Back to results" rules apply.

A note is ignored when the visitor reached the development again from another page.

**Coming back.** When the browser keeps the list in its back/forward cache, the list returns exactly as it was. When it doesn't, the list scrolls the card to the middle of the screen and puts the keyboard focus on it (the focus ring shows only to visitors who opened the card from the keyboard); if the card is gone, it returns to where the visitor was. The browser's own scroll restoration is paused only for that return. The way back is kept in the list's own history entry, so it still works after the visitor has opened cards on other lists in between. With `view_transitions` on, Chrome and Edge hold the list's first paint until that card has loaded and centre it before showing the page, so even a slow phone opens the list at the card rather than at the top.

**The photo transition (`view_transitions`).** In Chrome and Edge, the card's photo grows into the development's main photo and shrinks back on the way back; the rest of the page cross-fades. Only the photo moves. It is skipped with *Reduce motion*, *Data Saver*, on 2G and on phones with 2 GB of memory or less; other browsers load the page as before. With the switch on, development pages show the main photo first, at 3:2 like the cards: full width at the top on phones and tablets, 7 of 12 columns beside the name and form from 1000px with photos 2 and 3 under it, two across (each opens the viewer there), held in view while the text beside it scrolls. The "Project render" label moves from the photo to the gallery viewer (the disclaimer already mentions renders). Ad landing pages are never changed.

**Prefetch (`prefetch`).** When the pointer rests on a card (or a finger touches it), Chrome fetches the development page in advance, so it opens at once. Never on *Data Saver* or 2G, and nothing is prerendered, so analytics run once. A fetch is not counted as a view; when the visitor opens the fetched page, the page counts it then (`POST /wp-json/block/v1/view`, which exists only while `prefetch` is on, counts published development pages, not editors' own visits, and at most once per visitor address and development every 10 minutes). The visitor's address is Cloudflare's `CF-Connecting-IP` only when the request really came from one of Cloudflare's published addresses (listed in `blockke_bkn_is_cf()`), otherwise the connection's own, so a request sent straight to the server can't make up a new address each time. Known limit, accepted: visitors who share one public address, as many do on Kenyan mobile networks, count once per development every 10 minutes when they open it from a prefetched copy; direct visits and reloads count every time as before.

**Turning it on.** Both switches are off. Editors can preview the photo-first hero on any development page with `?bke_vt=1` (and `?bke_vt=0`); the transition itself needs the switch, as the parameter is not carried from page to page. Logged-in editors always get the list note on development pages, so previews of the new lists work end to end. With every v1.6 switch off, development and landing pages are byte for byte as in v1.5 for visitors.

**Tracking.** `context_beds` (bedrooms preset from the card; once, not again on a reload, Back or Forward) and `back_to_results` with `source` (index, area, segment, search or other).

## Enquiries

Every form posts to `/wp-json/block/v1/development-lead`, which:

- saves the enquiry in the option `blockke_dev_leads` (the last 200) before Zoho and the email, so a slow or failing step can't lose it;
- emails the enquiry addresses (`notify_emails`; on block.ke loyd@block.ke, sales@block.ke and loydmokaya@gmail.com) and the listing's agent;
- forwards the lead to Zoho through the popup snippet's Web-to-Lead connection, once its tokens are set;
- tags the source (Google Ads, Meta, organic and so on) using the same rules as the popup;
- fires the Google Ads lead conversion (`window.blockkeLeadConversion`), GA4 `generate_lead` and Meta `Lead`.

If the listing has a brochure, either a PDF link in the box or a PDF attached to the listing, it opens straight after the visitor sends their details.

**Optional questions after a quick enquiry.** The hero and pop-up forms ask only for a name and phone number. Once the lead is in, the thank-you panel offers three one-tap questions: which home, when they would like to buy (within 3 months, 3–12 months, just exploring) and whether they are buying to live in or invest. Answers are added to the same lead in `blockke_dev_leads` and emailed as "[Block] More details: …"; add them to the Zoho lead by hand, as Web-to-Lead can't update a lead. Skipping them loses nothing. The full enquiry form asks the timeline as well. The questions are not a separate form, so GA4 and Meta form tracking don't count the lead twice.

**Spam and AutoFill.** Each form has a hidden field that people never see and bots fill in. A send that fills it is answered as if it worked, but nothing is emailed or saved as a lead; the last 30 are kept in `blockke_dev_spam` in case one was a person. Before v1.3.1 the field was called `company`, which browsers' AutoFill fills in, so enquiries from visitors using AutoFill were dropped without a trace. It now has a name AutoFill leaves alone and isn't drawn at all. When a send fails, the form shows the error code (for example "error 403") before the WhatsApp link, so the cause can be traced.

## Getting around: search, similar homes and the unit switcher (v1.4)

**Search homes.** A "Search" button sits next to Share at the top of every development page, and a search icon sits in the desktop section bar. It opens a short search already filled in from the listing, for example Buy · Westlands · 2 bed · KES 12M–18M. "Show homes" opens the site's own search results (`/advanced-search/?location=Westlands&property_action_category[]=for-sale&componentsbeds=2&price_low=…&price_max=…`, plus `completion` for sales). "Or ask an advisor to shortlist for me" turns the same search into an enquiry. On phones the browser's back button closes the search. Without JavaScript the button is a plain link to the filled-in search.

**Studios (v1.4.2).** Bedrooms in the search start at *Studio*. Because the site search does not filter on 0 bedrooms, picking Studio searches the site's Studio category instead (`property_category[]=studio-apartments`, set by `studio_cat`; `''` removes the option). Studio pages link the same way: "See all studio homes in Westlands" under Also consider, and the Studio chip in a development's Residences. Studios are stored as 0 bedrooms, and a studio needs the Studio category to appear in these results.

**Also consider.** On a home's page (a unit or a single listing), this shows up to 6 homes with the same number of bedrooms. It picks them in this order:
1. Homes in the same area within 25% of the price, closest in price and size first.
2. Homes in the same area at any price.
3. Homes in the nearest neighbouring areas (config `neighbours`).
4. Homes with one bedroom more or fewer in the area.
5. The next ring of areas, only while fewer than 3 have been found.

It shows one home per development, and never this development or sold, let, commercial, office or per-sq-ft-priced listings. A villa or house (v1.4.4) is compared only with villas and houses, and when nothing turns up nearby, the developments shown are the villas and houses closest in price rather than the newest developments. When nothing matches, the message names the development its own homes were left out of ("No other 3-bedroom homes in Limuru outside Tilisi Views listed right now"). Rentals are compared with rentals. A studio stored as a 1-bedroom is recognised from its title, or from a size under 500 sq ft when the title gives no bedroom count (so a compact "1 Bedroom" stays a 1-bed). Listings for a range of homes ("2–4 Bed Lofts", "Studio & 1 Bed") are left out. Under the cards are "See all 2-bed homes in Westlands" (the site search), "Refine search" and "Compare these with an advisor". Development pages show similar developments instead. If fewer than 3 matches turn up, the block shows developments in the area. If those are short too, it shows the one or two homes it did find. Only when there are none does it say so and offer "Ask an advisor to find one". `bke_dev_similar` (listing IDs) puts chosen listings first on every kind of page and is never dropped. "Hide Also consider" turns the block off for one listing. The block is cached for each listing. It is rebuilt whenever a published listing's price, bedrooms, area, status or photo changes, a listing is published, unpublished or deleted, or an area or project is renamed. Drafts don't count. The cache reads only the few fields it needs, not each listing's view statistics.

**Unit switcher and breadcrumb.** A unit's page shows a row of the development's homes (1 Bed 6.5M · 2 Bed 13.5M · 3 Bed 18M · All homes). The breadcrumb reads Home / For Sale / Westlands / Golden Mansion / 2 Bedroom Apartment; on phones it shortens to "‹ Golden Mansion". A visitor who came from the site's search or a listings page sees "‹ Back to results" instead, which survives hops between the development's homes. Under the residences table, "See all homes for sale in Westlands" follows the bedroom filter ("Comparing? See other 2-bed homes in Westlands").

**Main area.** A listing tagged with several areas uses its development's area, else the one with the most listings. The Golden Mansion 2-bed, tagged Industrial Area and Westlands, therefore reads Westlands everywhere: the hero, breadcrumb, similar homes and the enquiry email. Set "Main area (slug)" on a listing to choose it by hand.

**Ad landing pages** stay a closed funnel: no search, no similar homes and none of their database queries. They show "Not sure Golden Mansion is the one? Send me 3 alternatives", which opens the enquiry form ("Alternatives request" in the email). Only after the visitor has enquired does the thank-you panel add one link: "While you wait: see other 2-bed homes in Westlands".

**Rolling it out.** `auto_units` (default off) switches the layout on for unit listings for sale without ticking each one. Set it to `true` for all of them, or to a list of area slugs, e.g. `array( 'westlands' )`, to go area by area.

**Tracking.** GA4 / dataLayer events:
- `search_open`, `search_submit` (with the fields and how many the visitor changed), `search_shortlist`;
- `similar_view` (once), `similar_click` (with position and tier), `similar_see_all`;

The new enquiry sources `alternatives`, `search-shortlist`, `compare` and `find` are named in the lead email.

**Settings** (option `blockke_dev_page`):
- similar homes: `similar_count` (6), `similar_min` (3), `price_band` (0.25), `dev_price_band` (0.35), `neighbours`, `pool_cap` (200), `sim_ttl` (12 hours), `similar_units`;

## Install and roll back

1. Open **WPCode → Code Snippets**. To install from this file instead, add a new PHP snippet and paste the file without its first `<?php` line.
2. Set *Insert method* to Auto Insert and *Location* to Run Everywhere. Turn on **Active** and click **Update**.
3. Check an off-plan property: the payment plan calculator replaces the mortgage one, *Features & Amenities* shows the tiles, and *Address* shows *What's nearby*. Then preview a development with `?bke_layout=1`.

To roll back, deactivate the snippet. Every page returns to the theme's own template and calculator. If area pages were on, also save **Settings → Permalinks** once, so the segment URLs (`/property_area/westlands/homes-for-sale/`) stop answering with the theme's area archive.

**Upgrading.** Each version is staged as its own snippet with a lower priority number than the one before, so it runs first: v1.3 at priority 4, v1.1 at 5, v1.0 at 10. (v1.2 was never switched on; its snippet became v1.3.) The whole file is wrapped in `if ( ! function_exists( 'blockke_dev_config' ) )`, so whichever version runs first is the one used, and the others skip themselves. To upgrade, switch the new snippet on, and it takes over at once. Then switch the older ones off, or keep the newest of them as a fallback. To go back, switch the new one off.

WPCode runs a cached copy of each active snippet, refreshed when a snippet is saved in WPCode. A snippet edited through the API stays as it was on the site until someone clicks **Update** on it, or on any other snippet.

## Enquiries inbox (`block-enquiries-inbox.php`)

A separate WPCode PHP snippet that keeps every website enquiry inside WordPress, whether or not the emails arrive. It adds an **Enquiries** page (administrators only) and a "Latest enquiries" box on the dashboard:

- **Website enquiries:** the price-check popup's leads and the development and landing page leads, newest first, with WhatsApp, call and email links, and whether the email was handed over and what Zoho said.
- **Emails the site sent:** every email WordPress tries to send, the theme's contact forms included, with its text and whether WordPress could hand it to the mail server. Password, login and code emails are listed without their text.
- **Zoho CRM replies:** what Zoho answered for each lead, to find out why leads don't appear in the CRM.
- **Download all as CSV**, and **Send a test email** to the enquiry addresses.

Enquiries the theme's contact forms send to an agent are copied (Bcc) to the enquiry addresses. Those addresses are the price-check popup's `notify_emails` (option `blockke_buyer_popup`), also used by the development pages (option `blockke_dev_page`). On block.ke both are set to loyd@block.ke, sales@block.ke and loydmokaya@gmail.com.

**Emails still need an email service.** WordPress hands email to the web server, and many inboxes reject or hide those messages. Install WP Mail SMTP, or a similar plugin, and connect Gmail, Google Workspace, Zoho Mail or Brevo; then use *Send a test email*. Until then, the Enquiries page shows a warning.

Tested on the local WordPress copy: leads from both sources in date order, the agent copy, failed and sent emails, private subjects, Zoho replies, the page, the dashboard box, the CSV (formulas neutralised, phones kept as text, refused without its security token) and the test email.
