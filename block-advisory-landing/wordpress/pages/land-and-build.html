      Land checks

      Eight questions to answer before any deposit

      Ask them of the seller, your advocate and a licensed surveyor, and keep every answer on paper before any money moves.

      1. The official search

        Your advocate runs it. Does the seller’s ID match the name on the title? See the result before any deposit.

      2. Your own title, or a mother title

        A plot sold only on a mother title can leave you waiting for your own title; ask which title you will get. 7 Jamii Bora (Kisaju, Kajiado) homeowners who moved in from 2008 still had no individual titles when they petitioned the Senate in December 2020. 8

      3. Freehold or leasehold

        Ask which, and on a lease, how many years are left; lenders can set a minimum. Non-citizens can hold land on leases of up to 99 years. 13

      4. Beacons on the ground

        A licensed surveyor confirms the beacons and dimensions. Ask whether the boundaries are fixed by a deed plan or shown only approximately on the Registry Index Map. 10

      5. Subdivision and change of user

        Ask for the county’s subdivision approval and, if the land was agricultural, the consent and change-of-user approvals. 11

      6. Land Control Board consent

        If the land is agricultural land in a land control area, the sale needs Land Control Board consent or it is void. 12

      7. The estate’s rules and charges

        The rules, the service charge and the design guidelines, read before you choose the plot. 6

      8. What is built, and what is promised

        Roads, water, power, sewer, the wall and the gate: what is in today, and what is still planned. A developer’s verbal promises may not bind them unless they are in the written agreement. 14

      Why the search comes first

      The Ministry of Lands has estimated that over 10% of titles in circulation may be fraudulent, as reported by Kenyans.co.ke. 9 It is one reason the official search comes before any payment.

      Dates

      The Law Society reported Ardhisasa, the online land registry, unavailable from 1 September 2026, and we had seen no notice of its return by 13 October 2026. 16 So we don’t promise search or transfer dates; your advocate tracks them.

      FAQs

      Questions buyers ask

      If yours isn’t here, ask it in the form.

      Does Block build the house?

      No. A vetted, NCA-registered contractor builds under a written contract with you, and Block Construction manages, inspects and certifies the work on your behalf. The person checking the work should never be the person paid to do it.

      Who pays Block?

      The first consultation is free. On the build, Block Construction’s fees depend on the scope and length of the project; on the purchase, some work is covered by the transaction and some is an agreed fee. Either way, the fee is agreed in writing first. On some estates the developer pays Block for introducing buyers; we tell you which, in writing, before you commit.

      Can you check a plot I found elsewhere?

      Tell us about it, and we will say plainly what Block can look at with you and what needs your advocate or a licensed surveyor. The official search always goes through your advocate.

      What counts as “gated” on this page?

      We list an estate as gated only where its listing states a gate, a perimeter wall or fence, or controlled access. Where part of that is still being built, we say so on the card. Category labels and feature tags don’t count.

      What is stamp duty on a plot?

      Stamp duty is 4% in gazetted towns and municipalities and 2% elsewhere, charged on the higher of the price or the government valuation. Your advocate confirms which applies to your plot (Ministry of Lands directive of 5 Apr 2024; as at 16 Oct 2026).

      Can I do this from abroad?

      Yes. A power of attorney lets you complete without boarding a plane: once registered, it lets someone you appoint sign for you. During the build, Block Construction works on milestone reports with dated photos and video, payments only against certified work, one project lead on WhatsApp and one shared folder.

      Can I use my own architect?

      Yes. Block Construction briefs and coordinates the architect and engineers, whether that is our Block Architecture and Design team or your own. Read the estate’s design rules first: some estates offer pre-approved designs, and some review every plan through a committee.

      Which approvals will I need?

      Usually the estate’s own design approval where it has one, then county development permission before work starts, NCA project registration within 30 days of awarding the contract (NCA, as at 16 Oct 2026), NEMA where your project triggers it and, at the end, an occupation certificate. We confirm the exact list for your site at feasibility stage.

      Can I buy now and build later?

      It depends on two things. Some estates set their own rules on when you must build, so ask before you buy. And once you have county development permission, it lapses if work hasn’t started within three years (Physical and Land Use Planning Act 2019, section 64(1)).

      Can a non-citizen buy?

      Non-citizens can hold land on leases of up to 99 years (Constitution, Article 65; Land Registration Act, section 107(3)). Your advocate confirms what that means for a particular plot.

      What does the first consultation cost?

      Nothing. It is free and without obligation: a 15–30 minute call, and a written summary of the next steps if you want one. We respond the same day.

      Which areas do you cover?

      Block Construction works in Nairobi and its satellite towns, from Ruaka and Tatu City to Syokimau, Kitengela and Rongai. For projects further afield, talk to us first, and that includes the estates on this page near Thika, in Nyeri and in Kitale.
